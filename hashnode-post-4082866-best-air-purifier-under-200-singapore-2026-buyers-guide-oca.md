## What to look for under S$200

Three things matter more than brand at this price:

1. **Filter standard (HEPA-13 vs HEPA-12)** — True HEPA-13 captures 99.97% of particles down to 0.3 microns. The Sterra Moon is one of the few sub-S$200 units that hits the HEPA-13 spec.
2. **Room coverage in m²** — Singapore HDB rooms are typically 10-20 m² (bedroom) or 20-40 m² (living room). Pick a unit rated for at least your largest room.
3. **CADR (Clean Air Delivery Rate)** — Look for 200+ m³/h for bedroom use, 350+ m³/h for living room. Vendor specs are often optimistic; treat the number as a ceiling.

Skip: app ecosystems you do not need, voice assistants, PM2.5 laser sensors (nice but not essential at this price). Filter replacement cost is the real long-term cost — check filter prices before buying.

## Common mistakes

1. **Buying by brand, not by HEPA grade.** A cheap Dyson or Levoit sub-S$200 unit may only have HEPA-12. Sterra Moon and Xiaomi Smart 4 both hit True HEPA-13 at the same price.
2. **Ignoring room coverage.** A 20 m² unit in a 35 m² living room barely moves the PSI. Buy for your largest room.
3. **Forgetting filter replacement cost.** A S$189 purifier with S$80 filters every 12 months is more expensive long-term than a S$159 unit with S$45 filters. Check the consumable.
4. **Trusting CADR numbers blindly.** Asian-market specs are often measured at high fan speed. Realistic CADR is 60-70% of headline. Buy 30-50% more coverage than your room needs.
5. **Overlooking noise.** "Sleep mode" specs typically mean 25-30 dB. Anything above 35 dB will wake light sleepers. Check the dB rating, not just the wattage.

## FAQ

**Which air purifier under S$200 in Singapore is best for HDB bedrooms?**
For a typical 12-15 m² HDB bedroom, the Sterra Moon True HEPA-13 ($189) is overkill but quiet on low. The Levoit Core Mini ($99) or Xiaomi Smart Air Purifier 4 Lite ($129) are better-sized and quieter.

**Is HEPA-13 worth the upgrade over HEPA-12?**
For haze season (typically Aug-Oct), yes. HEPA-13 captures smaller particles including some bacteria and viruses. For everyday use without haze, HEPA-12 is fine.

**What about Dyson or Levoit?**
Dyson Purifier Big+Quiet starts at S$1,299 — well over budget. Levoit's sub-S$200 Core Mini uses HEPA-12, not HEPA-13. If you want Levoit's HEPA-13 line, expect to pay S$300+ in Singapore.

**How often do I need to change the filter?**
Every 12 months for HEPA-13 in typical Singapore use. During haze season, change every 6 months. Filter cost is S$45-S$80 per replacement depending on model.

**Are air purifiers worth it in Singapore?**
Yes for bedrooms (close-quarters, 8h exposure) and during haze season. For living rooms with open windows, mechanical ventilation helps more than a purifier. Close windows + run purifier for the highest measurable PSI drop.

**Do any of these have PM2.5 displays?**
The Sterra Moon and Xiaomi Smart Air Purifier 4 have PM2.5 laser sensor displays. Cheaper units (Levoit Core Mini, Honeywell P2) do not. Worth the upgrade if you want to see real-time air quality.

**Can I use these during haze season?**
Yes — that is the main use case. Pick a unit rated for 30+ m² for a living room. Run on high during peak PSI hours, low/auto overnight. Close all windows.
